\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Predicting somatic mutations from histology images is a weakly
supervised learning problem: a single binary label (mutated or
wild-type) is assigned to an entire gigapixel image containing
thousands of heterogeneous tissue regions, with no annotation of
which regions carry the mutation signal.
In lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD)---the most common lung cancer
subtype---identifying driver mutations such as EGFR or KRAS
determines whether a patient is eligible for targeted therapy.
The current gold standard, DNA sequencing, requires 7--21~days and
is unavailable in many hospitals.
Deep learning can predict mutations directly from routine
haematoxylin-and-eosin (H\&E) stained tissue slides, but
state-of-the-art methods require tens of thousands of proprietary
slides and provide no explanation of why a mutation is predicted---a
critical barrier to clinical trust.

This thesis asks: \emph{Can structured domain knowledge, encoded
through fuzzy set theory, be injected into a deep learning pipeline
to achieve competitive mutation prediction with fewer than 700
public slides while making the model's reasoning transparent?}
Seven research questions operationalise this goal across four
dimensions: pattern classification, mutation prediction,
interpretability, and knowledge-grounded explanation.
Following a design science research framework, three computational
artefacts are designed, implemented, and evaluated.

\textbf{Artefact~1 (FuzzyArcLoss~V2).}
In histology, tissue regions often exhibit gradual transitions
between morphological categories rather than sharp boundaries---a
tile may look partly glandular and partly solid.
Standard loss functions force a hard classification, penalising
all errors equally.
FuzzyArcLoss~V2 addresses this by introducing a fuzzy membership
function that adaptively reduces the angular margin penalty for
ambiguous samples, allowing the model to learn softer, more
biologically faithful representations.
The classifier is trained on 637 expert-annotated tissue tiles
(each with pixel-level spatial ground truth drawn by a pathologist)
and assigns every tile a probability vector over six histological
growth patterns.
Across 15~runs (5-fold $\times$ 3~seeds), FuzzyArcLoss~V2 achieves
macro-F1 $= 92.31\% \pm 2.04$, ranking first among 18~compared loss
functions and significantly outperforming the strongest baseline
($p = 0.001$, Cohen's $d = 1.00$).

\textbf{Artefact~2 (Pattern-Informed ABMIL).}
Standard attention-based multiple-instance learning (MIL) treats
each tile as an opaque feature vector and must discover
morphological structure from mutation labels alone.
This thesis proposes concatenating the pretrained visual embedding
(512 dimensions) with the 6-dimensional fuzzy pattern probabilities
from Artefact~1, producing an enriched 518-d tile representation
that explicitly encodes \emph{what} each tile looks like in
clinically validated terms.
A gated attention mechanism then learns which tiles contribute most
to the slide-level mutation prediction.
This explicit injection of histological knowledge as a structured
prior is a key novelty of the approach.

\textbf{Artefact~3 (Fuzzy Choquet MIL).}
Standard attention pooling aggregates tiles independently, ignoring
potential interactions between morphological categories.
The Choquet integral, operating on a learned fuzzy measure, captures
such interactions---for example, that the simultaneous presence of
two minority patterns within a tumour is more predictive of a
specific mutation than either pattern alone.
The fuzzy measure requires only 21~learnable parameters (6~singleton
Shapley values + 15~pairwise interaction indices) and is
intrinsically interpretable: its parameters directly reveal which
patterns and pattern combinations the model considers important,
without requiring any post-hoc explanation method.

All artefacts are evaluated on the largest public LUAD cohort
(687~slides, 668~patients, 6~target genes) using stratified
5-fold cross-validation with patient-level splits.
The pattern-informed ABMIL attains AUROCs of $0.717 \pm 0.058$
(TP53), $0.694 \pm 0.051$ (EGFR), $0.695 \pm 0.023$ (STK11),
and $0.610 \pm 0.071$ (KEAP1)---competitive with similarly-scaled
studies while providing a six-level interpretability framework
absent from all prior work: spatial attention maps, pattern overlays,
SHAP feature attribution, intrinsic Choquet Shapley values,
per-slide feature channel ablation, and fuzzy linguistic labels
for XAI outputs that prevent LLM hallucination of intensity terms.
The Fuzzy Choquet MIL emerges as the optimal method for KRAS
($0.609 \pm 0.059$) and RBM10 ($0.661 \pm 0.063$), confirming
that the Choquet branch adds gene-specific discriminative value.
A novel analytical finding is that the utility of pattern knowledge
is gene-dependent: embedding-only models suffice for genes with
strong visual correlates, while pattern-informed and Choquet models
benefit genes whose mutation signal is distributed across multiple
morphological categories.
Per-slide feature channel ablation---comparing separately trained
combined, embeddings-only, and patterns-only models on the same
tiles---reveals that \emph{high SHAP pattern attribution does not
imply positive contribution}: patterns measurably improve prediction
only for KRAS ($+7.6$\,pp) while actively degrading it for TP53
($-16.1$\,pp) and KEAP1 ($-44.7$\,pp), a dissociation between
attribution and utility that no prior system exposes.

A cross-cutting contribution is the empirical characterisation of
the \emph{genotype--phenotype gap} that fundamentally limits
histology-based mutation prediction.
The mutation labels used for training come from DNA sequencing and
indicate only \emph{whether} a patient carries a mutation, not
\emph{where} in the tissue its effect is visible---a patient-level
binary signal with no spatial correspondence to the image.
This is in stark contrast to the pattern classification stage, where
pathologist-drawn pixel-level annotations provide exact spatial
ground truth.
Consequently, only mutations that visibly reorganise tissue
architecture are reliably detectable from microscopy images; mutations
that operate at the molecular level without altering visible structure
remain inherently difficult.
This gap is compounded by co-mutations: most tumours harbour two or
more simultaneous driver mutations whose phenotypic effects
superimpose, making it impossible to attribute observed morphology to
any single gene.
The six-level interpretability framework---spatial attention,
pattern overlay, post-hoc SHAP attribution, intrinsic Choquet
Shapley values, per-slide feature channel ablation, and fuzzy
linguistic labels---makes these limitations transparent, enabling
clinicians to
judge when a prediction is trustworthy and when molecular testing is
required.
Crucially, the system's explanations are \emph{most valuable when
predictions fail}: for genes labelled Inconclusive
(AUROC~$< 0.70$), the SHAP decomposition and Choquet Shapley
values quantitatively explain \emph{why} the model lacks confidence,
distinguishing genes whose signal resides in sub-cellular texture
(embedding-dominated) from those where the six-pattern taxonomy
carries partial information (pattern-dominated).
This capacity to explain its own limitations---rather than merely
flagging low confidence---is absent from all prior mutation
prediction systems and represents a key contribution of this work.

A preliminary expert validation by a board-certified pathologist
reveals domain shift between the training cohort (ANORAK, UK) and the
inference cohort (TCGA, US), motivating an active learning mechanism
whereby pathologists can correct tile-level pattern classifications
and trigger incremental model refinement.

The resulting system, \lchai{}, integrates all artefacts into a
clinical decision-support prototype deployed on cloud infrastructure,
with an ontology-grounded explanation layer that links predicted
mutations to therapeutic targets through a curated biomedical
knowledge graph.
This thesis contributes the first integration of fuzzy-logic--encoded
histological domain knowledge into an attention MIL mutation
prediction pipeline, offering interpretability at multiple
granularities---from tile-level pattern classification to slide-level
Shapley-value decomposition---within a single, coherent framework.
While the system is not intended to replace molecular testing,
it can serve as a triage tool to prioritise patients for expedited
sequencing, as an educational resource for residents learning
morphology--genotype relationships, and as a reusable research
framework applicable to other cancer types with WHO-defined growth
pattern taxonomies.

\paragraph{Keywords.}
Fuzzy logic, angular margin loss, multiple-instance learning,
Choquet integral, Shapley values, histopathological patterns,
lung adenocarcinoma, mutation prediction, genotype--phenotype gap,
design science research, computational pathology, explainable AI.
